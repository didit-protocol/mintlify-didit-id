title: "Wallet security"
description: "How passkeys, consent, selective disclosure, and replay protection protect holder credentials."
---

The Didit holder wallet protects credentials with passkeys and gives holders a consent step before every presentation. It uses standards-based holder binding and request binding so verifiers can trust the presentation without receiving unnecessary claims.

## Passkey-secured access

The wallet uses passkeys (WebAuthn) for account access. Holders use a passkey-capable browser, platform authenticator, or security key to sign in and approve wallet use.

<Note>
  Credentials are protected with your passkeys, and wallet presentations still use the standards-based holder proof required by OpenID4VCI and OpenID4VP.
</Note>

## Consent before disclosure

Before a presentation, the wallet shows:

- The relying party requesting proof.
- The credential type.
- The issuer.
- The exact claims requested.

The wallet presents only the disclosures the holder approves and only the claims the verifier requested.

## Selective disclosure

Selective disclosure keeps unnecessary claims out of the verifier response.

For example, a credential can include `over_18`, `birth_date`, and `country`. If a relying party requests `over_18`, the wallet can present `over_18` without disclosing `birth_date` or `country`.

## Replay protection

OpenID4VP presentations are bound to a relying-party audience and a nonce. A captured presentation cannot be reused for a different verifier or a different request.

Credential offers and presentation requests are also designed as limited-use flows. If an offer expires or is already claimed, ask the issuer to send a new one.

## What verifiers receive

When verification succeeds, the relying party receives:

- The final `verdict`.
- The disclosed claims the holder approved.
- The verification checks, such as signature, holder binding, audience, nonce, expiry, revocation, and trust policy.

The relying party does not receive undisclosed credential values.
