title: "Trust and trusted issuers"
description: "Learn why verifiers need trust policy before they accept credentials."
---

A verifier is the system checking a credential. An issuer is the organization that created it. A verifier should not accept every signed credential.

A fake company can sign a fake student ID, just like anyone can print a badge at home. The signature proves who made the credential, but the verifier still needs to decide whether that issuer is allowed.

In Didit ID, that decision is a trust policy. The policy can trust a whole framework, trust individual issuers, or block an issuer inside a framework.

## Trust policy

A trust policy answers, "Who do we believe for this proof?"

For example, a campus door may trust State University for student ID credentials. It should not trust a random website that claims to issue student IDs.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Choose the trust context">
    Pick a trust framework. A framework is the rulebook or governed group for the kind of credential you want to verify.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Trust a framework or issuer">
    Trust an entire framework when its governance is enough. Trust a single issuer when you want a narrower policy.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Block exceptions">
    If you trust a framework but do not accept one issuer inside it, block that issuer.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Apply it to a relying party">
    A relying party can use the account policy or have its own policy for the frameworks and issuers it accepts.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Verify presentations">
    When a wallet presents a credential, Didit ID checks the digital seal, lifecycle status, and trust policy.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  A credential can have a valid digital seal and still fail because the issuer is not accepted by the trust policy.
</Note>

## Didit Network

The **Didit Network** is a governed trust framework for issuers on the Didit platform. New accounts trust it by default, so the live demo works immediately.

You can turn it off when you want explicit trust. Then your verifier accepts only the frameworks and issuers you choose.

## Trust frameworks

A trust framework is a named rulebook for a credential ecosystem. It groups issuers under shared governance.

Electronic Identification, Authentication and Trust Services (eIDAS) is a European Union trust framework for electronic identity and trust services.

Mobile driving license (mDL) is a trust framework family for digital driver's licenses based on mobile license standards.

<Tip>
  Behind the scenes, Didit ID uses industry standards that let wallets, issuers, and verifiers work together: SD-JWT VC for credentials, OpenID4VCI for issuing, and OpenID4VP for presenting. The [Concepts](/concepts/sd-jwt-vc) pages explain each one.
</Tip>
